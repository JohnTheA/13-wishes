Attune

The titration loop for hormone therapy.
Turning “dose by feel” into a data problem — US & Canada.

Jiaen Yu · Founder

Problem

“Dose by feel.”

Clinical guidelines are explicit: menopause hormone therapy is titrated by her symptoms — not blood levels.1

Yet no system records those symptoms.

Problem

She waits. Her doctor guesses.

3–6 mo
of trial & error to find a stable dose — 4–6 weeks per change2
~50%
of women quit the therapy within the first year3
62%
stop abruptly, against medical advice — and 84% relapse4
≈1M women start this therapy every year in the US + Canada (est. — stock-and-flow calc, sources p.17), on prescriptions up 86% in four years.5,6
Problem

Symptoms last 7.4 years.
Treatment dies in year 1.7

Titration is a craft run on recall.
It should be a data problem.

Solution

A monitored season.
Not another tracker.

Day 1
She snaps her prescription label — regimen logged in 30 seconds. Apple Health connects as the base.
Every day
A 30-second conversation asks what her clinician would ask. “Day 18 on this dose — most women see this settle by week 4–5.”
Every visit
Her doctor gets one page — the dose-response curve, formatted as their own follow-up checklist. Record replaces recall.
Week ~20
Stable dose. She graduates. The record stays.
Why it works

The loop is already proven.

In a randomized JAMA trial, structured symptom reporting with alerts kept cancer patients on therapy 2 months longer — and extended survival 5.2 months.8

Sources & fact base
1The Menopause Society (NAMS), 2022 Hormone Therapy Position Statement — dose individualized to symptom relief; routine hormone-level testing not recommended.
2Clinical consensus ranges; assessment 4–6 wks post-change, full effect ≤12 wks (NAMS-aligned patient guidance).
3Menopause / PubMed 10614674 — 12-month discontinuation 48% (younger) – 62% (older); side-effects the top stated reason (64–87%).
4Bunnewell et al., BJOG (2025) systematic review, 69 studies — 62.4% stopped abruptly despite 91.6% of clinicians advising taper; 84.4% symptom recurrence.
5Epic Research (2026) — HRT prescribing +86%, Q2 2021 → Q4 2025.
6Truveta Research (Feb 2026) — 1 in 20 US women 45–54 hold an estrogen prescription; acceleration after FDA removed the boxed warning (2025). ~1M/yr new starts is a founder estimate (stock-and-flow: ~2.75M US users [JAMA 2024: ~5% of postmenopausal women] × 48–62% yr-1 discontinuation, growing stock; sensitivity 0.6–1.2M; claims-data validation in progress).
7Avis et al., JAMA Internal Medicine (2015), SWAN study, n=1,449 — median total VMS duration 7.4 years.
8Basch et al., JAMA (2017), RCT n=766 — web-based symptom reporting + nurse alerts: +5.2 mo median overall survival; ~2 mo longer on chemotherapy.
9CMS 2026 Physician Fee Schedule — RTM expansion (new CPT 98985/98984/98979); ~$94/patient/mo typical full-engagement RTM (98977+98980, national averages).
10Apple WWDC25 — HealthKit Medications API (read medications & dose events); iOS 27 perimenopause/menopause features (Apple Newsroom / MacRumors, Jun 2026).
11Veozah (fezolinetant) FDA approval 2023, Astellas — incl. OPTION-VMS phase-IV RWE study; Lynkuet (elinzanetant), Bayer, 2025.
12PANS Menopause Management Service (fee-based) & NS pharmacist MHT prescribing; MOMENT trial, Dalhousie, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07618039 (start Jul 1 2026).
13BC Ministry of Health / BC Family Doctors — MHT coverage effective Mar 2026.
14AARP survey (via GoodRx Research, 2025) — women spend ~$13B/yr out-of-pocket on menopause symptoms, >$10B on non-medical products.
